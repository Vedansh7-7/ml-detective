Death Cap, Dinner, or Doubt

Find the annotator whose labels can't be trusted.

A foraging app is about to tell hikers which mushrooms are dinner. Its five teachers don’t all agree.

Play this case →

The story

Every autumn the Hollowmere Mycological Society sends volunteers into the damp beechwoods of the Lake District with baskets, knives and clipboards. This year they logged nearly fifteen hundred specimens, each one examined, sniffed, spore-printed and given a verdict at the village hall on Thursday nights.

Society chair Winifred Oakes has struck a deal with a small startup: the survey will train SafeCap, a phone app that looks at a mushroom and tells a walker whether it belongs in the pan. Twenty thousand people have already pre-registered.

The developer, Rafi Castellano, says the model is mostly fine but “strangely confident about exactly the wrong things.” Winifred knows what that means in her hobby. A mistake here is not a one-star review. It is a hospital bed, a liver transplant list, or worse.

She has sent you the full survey export along with a jar of her chanterelle chutney, which she promises is perfectly safe. Five volunteers built this table together over one wet autumn. Something in how it was recorded will poison the app if nobody catches it. Find it before launch.

What you'll practise

  • crosstabs
  • comparing groups of labellers
  • label noise in training data

The fault is a relationship: a feature against the target, against time, or against another source. You write real pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn code in a notebook that runs in your browser. Nothing to install.

The data

Specimens collected by five volunteers over one autumn, with field characteristics and a toxic / edible verdict, exported to train a mushroom-identification app. One thing in this survey can’t be trusted. Find it. 1480 rows, loaded as df.

ColumnWhat it holds
specimen_idint, unique specimen number
found_ontext, ISO date the specimen was collected
identified_bytext, volunteer who examined the specimen and recorded the verdict
cap_diameter_cmfloat, cap diameter in cm
stem_height_cmfloat, stem height in cm
gill_spacingtext, close / crowded / distant
spore_printtext, spore print colour
smelltext, dominant smell (none, anise, almond, earthy, foul, fishy)
bruisestext, yes if the flesh bruises when handled
ringtext, yes if the stem has a ring
toxicint, 1 = toxic, 0 = edible (the target)
