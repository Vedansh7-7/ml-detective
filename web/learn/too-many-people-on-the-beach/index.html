Too Many People on the Beach

Check whether IDs are really unique.

A sold-out beach festival let in more people than it sold tickets to. The gate log knows how.

Play this case →

The story

Saltpan Sound is three nights of music on Morjim beach in North Goa, capped by the police at a strict headcount. Every wristband carries a barcode, and every barcode is scanned once at the gate. Gate lead Rhea Fernandes ran the scanners herself, and they never stopped beeping green.

On the final night a police inspector counted heads, then counted again, and the numbers did not match what the promoter, Kabir Sethi, had sold. Kabir now faces a hearing and a fine that could end the festival for good.

Rhea has exported the entry scan log: one line per wristband that walked through a gate. She believes the answer is sitting in there in plain sight, and the hearing is on Thursday. Find what let the extra people onto the sand.

What you'll practise

  • duplicated()
  • nunique() against row counts
  • tracing repeated records

The fault lives in one column. Summary statistics, value counts and a histogram will get you there. You write real pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn code in a notebook that runs in your browser. Nothing to install.

The data

The gate scanner log from a three-night beach festival: one row per wristband admitted, joined to its ticket sale. One thing in this log explains the crowd nobody paid for. Find it. 2399 rows, loaded as df.

ColumnWhat it holds
ticket_idstring, barcode printed on the wristband
purchase_datedate string, YYYY-MM-DD, when the ticket was sold
tiercategorical, Day Pass, Weekend or VIP
price_inrint, price paid in rupees (early-bird prices are lower)
buyer_statecategorical, Indian state of the buyer
gatecategorical, entry gate where the wristband was scanned
scan_timedatetime string, YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM of the entry scan
