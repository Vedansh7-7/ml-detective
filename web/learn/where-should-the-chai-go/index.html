Easy case · data forensics in Python

Where Should the Chai Go?

Clean up inconsistent category labels.

A chai startup is about to bet its savings on the wrong place. The board deck says so, anyway.

Play this case →

The story

Kulhad & Co. sells clay-cup chai through an app, and after nine months of sign-ups the founders are ready to open their first real flagship kiosk. Co-founder Tanvi Hegde has a lease draft on her desk in Pune. Her partner, Arjun Nair, keeps muttering that half their regulars seem to live somewhere else.

The investor, a retired banker named Mrs. Lobo, has one rule: the kiosk goes wherever the most customers already are. The board deck's bar chart points firmly at Pune, the tallest bar by a clear margin, and she plans to sign the cheque on Monday.

Arjun has slipped you the raw sign-up export behind that chart. Nobody has checked it since the app went live. Before anyone signs a five-year lease, find the thing in this file that could send the kiosk to the wrong place.

What you'll practise

  • value_counts()
  • normalising text categories
  • grouping before you count

The fault lives in one column. Summary statistics, value counts and a histogram will get you there. You write real pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn code in a notebook that runs in your browser. Nothing to install.

The data

Every customer who signed up on the chai app in its first nine months, with where they are and what their first month looked like. One thing in this export would lead a decision badly astray. Find it. 1800 rows, loaded as df.

ColumnWhat it holds
signup_idint, unique id
signup_datedate string, YYYY-MM-DD
citystring, city the customer entered at sign-up
age_bandcategorical, self-reported age range
referral_sourcecategorical, how they heard about the app
app_platformcategorical, Android or iOS
first_order_inrint, value of the first order in rupees (the app has a 90-rupee minimum order)
orders_first_monthint, number of orders in the first 30 days
