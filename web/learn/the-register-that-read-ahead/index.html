The Register That Read Ahead

Find a feature recorded after the outcome.

A lost steamer’s passenger list guesses who came home with spooky precision. Paper isn’t supposed to see the future.

Play this case →

The story

In April 1913 the steamer Carrow Belle went down in fog off Cape Race with 1,310 souls aboard. A century later the Halifax Harbourfront Maritime Museum is building an exhibit around her, and curator Maeve Lindqvist wants one wall to answer a hard question honestly: what actually decided who lived?

Her volunteer analyst, Jonah Achebe, digitised the shipping company’s register and trained a kiosk model on it. Visitors type in a class, an age, a port, and the screen tells them whether they would have made it home. In testing, it has almost never been wrong.

Maeve is not celebrating. Descendants of the passengers are flying in for opening night on Friday, and she refuses to show them a machine that is right for the wrong reasons. “That register was kept by clerks,” she says, “not by fortune tellers.”

She has handed you Jonah’s table, all 1,310 rows of it, with a pot of museum coffee and a polite warning about the Friday deadline. One thing in it is carrying information that no clerk could have written down on the morning she sailed. Find it before the doors open.

What you'll practise

  • groupby on missing vs present values
  • reasoning about when data was collected
  • leakage in classification

The fault is a relationship: a feature against the target, against time, or against another source. You write real pandas, NumPy and scikit-learn code in a notebook that runs in your browser. Nothing to install.

The data

A synthetic reconstruction of a fictional 1913 steamer’s passenger register, one row per passenger, prepared for a survival-prediction exhibit. One thing in this table can’t be trusted as a predictor. Find it. 1310 rows, loaded as df.

ColumnWhat it holds
passenger_idint, unique register number
pclassint, ticket class (1 = first, 2 = second, 3 = third)
sextext, female / male
agefloat, age in years (blank where the clerk left it empty)
sibspint, siblings or spouses travelling with the passenger
parchint, parents or children travelling with the passenger
farefloat, ticket price in pounds sterling
embarkedtext, port of embarkation (S = Southampton, C = Cherbourg, Q = Queenstown)
boattext, boat code noted in the company register (blank if none)
survivedint, 1 if the passenger survived the sinking, 0 if not (the target)
